## What real winning bakery / artisan-food sites do
1. **Photography is the spine.** The product is the appetite; warm, real, close-up
   imagery of the bake carries the page. (Tartine, Tartelette, artisan-bakery SOTDs.)
2. **Editorial serif display + generous type scale.** Big confident headlines, lots of
   air, hand-made warmth in the type (matches Boska's expressive humanist serif).
3. **Material warmth, not slick glass.** Paper grounds, flour grain, warm ovenlight,
   wood. The opposite of cold SaaS.
4. **A daily-bake / ritual narrative.** Winners tell the "we start at dawn" story; it
   makes freshness believable and emotional.
5. **Menu as a designed object** (ledger rows, named sections in the kitchen's own
   language) instead of a generic card grid.

## What to AVOID (category cliche, per anti_rules food bans)
- brown-on-cream "rustic template", fork-and-knife icon, faux-chalkboard menu
- generic food-stock carousel as the only idea
- wrong-community imagery (rejected a Chinese-menu interior shot during sourcing)

## Translation into THIS build (inside the lane)
- Boska display at every heading + a glaze-swept wordmark
- alternating horizontal bands: flour / paper / crust-brown / dark espresso
- real downloaded panaderia photography (conchas, bolillo, baker at the racks, pastel
  cakes, wheat-lit interior), each shot used once and labeled to what it depicts
- the daily-bake narrative ("Desde las 6 de la manana") drives the section order
- menu rendered as a warm ledger (pan dulce / pan salado / pasteleria / cafe), in Spanish
